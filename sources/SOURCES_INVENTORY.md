## 8. Three-Layer Evidentiary Architecture & Causal Framework

To ensure flawless econometric reasoning and prevent over-claiming, the paper organizes the empirical narrative into three distinct evidentiary tiers:

```
[Layer 1: Global Shock Transmission]
EIA (2024, 2026): Strait of Hormuz transits 20.7-20.9 mb/d (~20% global petroleum liquids).
84%-89% destined for Asia; ~75% has zero physical bypass pipeline alternative.
        │
        ▼
[Layer 2: Indian Macroeconomic & Physical Vulnerability]
PPAC (2023-2026): India crude import dependency = 87.4% to 88.9% (POL consumption basis).
Canara Bank (2025): 49.79% of Indian crude imports physically transit Hormuz (2.41 mb/d).
DEA (2026): 46.9% regional import share from Middle East; DGCI&S: HS 2709 is 19.84% of import bill.
        │
        ▼
[Layer 3: Domestic Economic & Microstructural Transmission]
ICRA (2026) & MoPNG: Geopolitical risk -> Freight/insurance surges, acquisition cost spikes,
and downstream OMC marketing margin squeeze (politically regulated pump prices).
        │
        ▼
[Empirical Econometric Testing (Research Contribution)]
Does this geopolitical supply uncertainty transmit into intraday equity pricing?
Result: Gap fade win rate collapses from 64.9% to 47.9% (h = -0.347, p < 0.001);
disproportionately concentrated in energy/OMCs (DiD β₃ = -8.10 pp).
```

1. **Global Shock Transmission (Layer 1)**: Grounded in EIA data establishing the systemic indispensability of the Strait of Hormuz.
2. **Indian Vulnerability (Layer 2)**: Grounded in PPAC, Canara Bank, DEA, and DGCI&S statistics proving extreme physical import dependency and geographic concentration.
3. **Economic & Margin Transmission (Layer 3)**: Grounded in ICRA and Ministry reports establishing how tanker risk and crude spikes compress refinery margins and elevate macro discount rates.
4. **Econometric Hypothesis & Finding**: The paper does not assume institutional reports prove equity market behavior; rather, the econometric models (Eq. 1 to Eq. 7) rigorously demonstrate that intraday market makers withdraw passive limit liquidity, breaking down the pre-crisis gap-reversal anomaly.
